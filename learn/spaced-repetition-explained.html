Spaced repetition, explained by the numbers用数字讲清间隔复习

Every flashcard app hides the same three numbers. Once you can read them, scheduling stops being magic and becomes arithmetic you can argue with.每个抽认卡应用背后都是同样的三个数字。一旦你会读它们，排程就不再是魔法，而变成你可以质疑的算术。

The problem spacing solves间隔复习解决的问题

Re-reading a page five times in one sitting feels productive and mostly is not. Each repetition arrives while the material is still fully available, so there is nothing to retrieve and little to strengthen. Spacing works by letting memory decay a little before you come back, so the act of remembering is effortful — and effortful successful retrieval is what durably strengthens a memory.一口气把一页读五遍，感觉很有产出，实际上大多没有。每一次重复到来时材料都还完整地在手边，于是没有什么需要提取，也就没什么被加固。间隔复习的做法是：让记忆先轻微衰减再回来，使"想起来"这件事变得费力——而费力却成功的提取，才是真正持久地加固记忆的东西。

Three numbers describe a card三个数字描述一张卡

Modern schedulers such as FSRS keep stability S — how many days until recall of this card falls to 90% — plus difficulty D on a 1–10 scale, and retrievability R, the probability you could recall it right now. S and D are updated by your grades. R is not stored at all: it is computed from how long it has been and how stable the card is.像 FSRS 这样的现代排程器为每张卡维护：稳定度 S——多少天后这张卡的记得率降到 90%；难度 D——1 到 10 的标度；以及可提取性 R——你此刻还能回忆起来的概率。S 与 D 由你的评分更新，R 根本不需要存储：它由"过了多久"和"卡有多稳定"算出来。

The equation you can actually use一个你真的用得上的公式

Recall decays as R(t, S) = (1 + 19/81 · t/S)^−0.5, where t is days since the last review. Two consequences follow immediately. First, S only stretches the time axis — doubling stability doubles how long any recall level takes to arrive, it does not lift the curve. Second, if you fix R at a target and solve for t, you get the next interval. That is the whole scheduling loop: grade the card, update S, solve for the day R hits your target.记忆按 R(t, S) = (1 + 19/81 · t/S)^−0.5 衰减，其中 t 是距上次复习的天数。由此立刻得到两个结论。第一，S 只是拉伸时间轴——稳定度翻倍，只会让任一记得率水平"晚一倍才到达"，并不会把曲线抬高。第二，把 R 固定在目标值再反解 t，就得到下一次间隔。整个排程循环就是这样：给卡评分、更新 S、解出"R 落到目标"的那一天。

Why intervals grow, and when they stop间隔为何增长，又何时停下

Each successful review multiplies stability, and the multiplier is largest when you review a card whose recall has already dropped somewhat — reviewing something you still know perfectly buys very little. This is why intervals grow quickly at first and then settle. It is also why "just review everything daily" is not the safe choice it feels like: it spends your time where the purchase is smallest.每一次成功复习都会把稳定度乘上去，而当你复习的是"记得率已经掉了一些"的卡时，这个乘数最大——去复习一件你还记得滚瓜烂熟的东西，几乎买不到什么。这就是间隔为什么先猛涨、后趋缓。这也解释了为什么"干脆每天都全部复习一遍"并不是感觉上那种安全选择：它把时间花在了收益最小的地方。

A lapse is not a reset — in FSRS在 FSRS 里，遗忘不等于清零

The classic SM-2 algorithm sends a failed card back to a one-day interval regardless of how long it had survived. FSRS instead computes a post-lapse stability from the stability you had already accumulated, so a mature card that slips resumes from a lower but non-zero base. If you have ever felt punished for one bad day, that difference is why.经典的 SM-2 算法会把答错的卡打回 1 天间隔，不管它此前存活了多久。FSRS 则依据你已累积的稳定度计算"遗忘后稳定度"，因此一张成熟卡失手一次，会从更低但非零的基线继续。如果你曾经觉得"一天没做好就被重罚"，差别就在这里。

What this does not tell you它没告诉你的事

These models describe retrieval of discrete items you have already understood. They say nothing about whether your cards are any good, and a scheduler cannot rescue a card that asks an ambiguous question. They also do not describe procedural skills — an instrument, a sport, pronunciation — which decay differently and usually far more slowly.这些模型描述的是"你已经理解了的离散条目的提取"。它们完全不涉及你的卡片质量好不好，而排程器救不了一张问题本身有歧义的卡。它们也不描述程序性技能——乐器、运动、发音——这些的衰减方式不同，而且通常慢得多。
