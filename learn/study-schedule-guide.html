Building a study schedule that survives contact做一份撑得住现实的学习计划

Most plans do not fail because someone was lazy. They fail because they were arithmetically impossible on the day they were written.多数计划失败，不是因为谁懒。而是因为它们在被写下的那一天，就已经在算术上不可能。

Size the load at steady state, never week one按稳态定负荷，绝不按第一周

Adding N new cards a day does not cost N reviews a day. Each card generates a future sequence, so today’s queue is the sum of every past card due today, and it keeps growing for months. Judge any new-card setting by its steady-state minutes per day. That number, not enthusiasm in week one, decides whether the plan exists in month three.每天加 N 张新卡，代价不是每天 N 次复习。每张卡都会生成一条未来序列，所以今天的队列是"所有过去的卡里今天到期的"之和，而且会持续增长数月。任何新卡设置都要用其稳态每日分钟数来判断。决定这个计划在第三个月是否还存在的，是这个数字，而不是第一周的热情。

Front-load new material把新材料前置

A card added late has no time left to accumulate stability before the date you need it. With a deadline, the binding question is not "can I see everything" but "does the last new card still get several spaced reviews". Check both; when they conflict, cut scope rather than compress spacing.临近才加入的卡，在你需要它之前已经没有时间累积稳定度了。有截止日期时，真正卡住你的问题不是"我能不能全部看一遍"，而是"最后一张新卡还能不能得到几次间隔复习"。两者都要检查；当它们冲突时，砍范围，而不是压缩间隔。

Scale the gaps to the horizon按跨度缩放间隔

There is no single optimal gap. Experimental work mapping the spacing ridgeline finds the best gap grows with how long you need to retain the material, so a test next week and a skill for next year genuinely want different schedules. Practically: for a near date, review sooner and more often; for durable knowledge, let intervals run.不存在单一最优间隔。绘制间隔脊线的实验工作发现，最优间隔随"你需要记多久"而增长，所以"下周考试"和"明年要用的技能"确实需要不同的排程。实际做法：日期临近就更早、更频繁地复习；追求持久知识则让间隔放开。

Order matters as much as amount顺序和数量一样重要

When consecutive problems all need the same method, you never practise choosing the method. In a three-month classroom experiment with 126 seventh-graders, the same problems in interleaved order beat blocked order with Cohen’s d = 0.42 after one day and 0.79 after thirty. Same workload, different order. Expect it to feel worse while you do it.当连续的题目都用同一个方法，你就从未练过"选方法"。在一项为期三个月、126 名七年级学生的课堂实验中，同样的题目按交错顺序排列，一天后的测试高出 Cohen’s d = 0.42，三十天后高出 0.79。工作量相同，顺序不同。请预期做的时候会更难受。

Place breaks, but do not fetishise the numbers安排休息，但别迷信具体数字

Sustained attention to one unchanging goal degrades; two brief switches away from a 50-minute vigilance task prevented that decline entirely, which is a real mechanism. The specific 25/5 pattern is convention: in a direct comparison with 94 students it showed no significant advantage over self-timed breaks, and a meta-analysis of 22 micro-break studies found consistent effects on fatigue but less consistent effects on performance. Take breaks; own the interval.长时间盯住一个不变的目标会导致表现下降；在 50 分钟的警觉任务中，两次短暂切离就完全阻止了这种下降——这是一个真实机制。但 25/5 这组具体数字只是约定：在一项 94 名学生的直接对比中，它并未显示出优于自定时休息的显著优势；一项涵盖 22 项微休息研究的元分析发现其对疲劳的效果稳定，对绩效的效果则不够一致。要休息，但间隔由你定。

Write the plan as a budget, then test it把计划写成预算，然后检验它

Convert every intention into numbers you can check before committing: new cards per day, steady-state minutes, required daily minutes to finish the reading, projected recall on the date. If those four numbers do not fit inside the days you actually have, the plan is already broken — and it is much cheaper to find out now than in week six.在承诺之前，把每一个打算都换成可检验的数字：每天多少新卡、稳态多少分钟、读完材料每天需要多少分钟、到那一天的预测记得率。如果这四个数字装不进你真正拥有的天数，这个计划就已经坏了——而现在发现，比第六周才发现便宜得多。
